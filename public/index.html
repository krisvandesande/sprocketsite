Whether you scan your own film or get them back from the lab, they always miss those essential EXIF data like camera, lens, film and ISO value. Sprocket EXIF Editor aims to fix that. Drag a full digital roll into our app, quickly edit either the basics, or go into expert mode, hit Save and all your photos will show up in order in your Photo Library of choice.

01 The river

Everything flows down the roll

It’s an app that was built with film scanning in mind. We want it to be an intuitive step between scanner and photo management systems. You drop in a folder of scanned film, select the kit you used and hit Let’s Go. In our main window you see your entire roll of photos in the “river”. We call it that because every photo you add information to flows through to the next photo. So the sequence of the roll will never be broken. You can go as detailed or as vague as you want: whether you have to add just a date or have detailled details of every single shot on the roll, we have you.

02 Your kit

It knows what your camera can do

Pick a camera and the lists follow: the lenses in your kit, the apertures that lens has, the speeds that shutter has. A Hasselblad lens runs from 1 second to 1/500 plus B. A Brownie has one speed, so Sprocket fills it in.

03 The file

A saved scan reads like a digital original

Hit Save and your scan looks to every other app as if it was shot digitally on your film camera. What the scanner or copy-stand camera left behind goes. Your own keywords, ratings and Lightroom edits stay.

TIFF, JPEG, PNG, HEIC, BMP and camera raws, including Hasselblad .fff and .3fr scans. Raws get an XMP sidecar next to the file. The pixels are never touched.

04 Our story

Photographer Kris Van de Sande loves shooting film, but hates the hassle it brings. As someone who likes to manage all his photo in the same date-based workflow, film scans always have been the odd-one-out. The lack of EXIF data makes sorting, searching and publishing frustrating. Through a complicated workflow through deprecated apps and Terminal commands, Kris was looking for a solution, and found it with Tech Creative Paul Sprangers.

Together they built Sprocket EXIF Editor, an intuitive step between scanner and photo management systems.
